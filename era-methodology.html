Era Classification Methodology

How the Tome Intelligence Dashboard classifies Vintage, Modern, and Current — and why it matters for the Tome Score.

PSA population counts mean different things depending on when a card was printed. A population of 500 PSA 10s on a 1999 card reflects twenty-five years of grading activity on a finite supply. The same population on a 2024 card means grading has barely started on a print run that may still be growing. Treating those numbers identically would systematically misprice scarcity, so the Tome Score applies era-specific population thresholds.

Why Evolving Skies is Current, not Modern: although printed in 2021, its population dynamics — massive print waves, ongoing grading influx, and alt-art chase economics — behave like today's market, not the sealed-supply-constrained sets of the 2010s. Era classification follows population behavior, not just the calendar.

How Era Affects the Tome Score

The population component (35% of the Tome Score) uses different scarcity thresholds per era. As of the current model, a PSA 10 population under 30 scores maximum for a vintage card; a current-era card needs a population under 100 to earn the same score, reflecting the earlier stage of its grading lifecycle. Full threshold tables are documented in the dashboard's formula reference.

Additionally, population scoring only activates on cards with a raw price of $10 or more. Below that floor, a low population typically reflects a card nobody bothers to grade rather than genuine scarcity, so the score treats population as neutral.

Reclassification

Era boundaries are reviewed periodically. Sets migrate as their population dynamics stabilize — what is Current today becomes Modern as grading influx slows. Changes are announced in the Tome Collective newsletter before taking effect in the dashboard.
